7 Cs of Effective Communication

Master the principles of clear and impactful communication

“True ease in writing comes from art, not chance; As those move easiest who have learned to dance” — Alexander Pope

With ever increasing competition in the market, companies need to stay on top with their products and services. They must maintain healthy relations with clients. Effective communication boosts productivity and helps achieve goals. The following principles help improve communication skills.

C — Clarity

Acquiring clarity in communication — speaking, reading, listening or writing — is essential. A communicator must be clear about the purpose. Words should define meaning precisely and avoid confusion.

Clarity ensures your message is understood exactly as intended and improves effectiveness.

C — Courtesy

Give respect and get respect. Showing gratitude or respect towards your audience or readers is priceless. A communicator must consider the feelings of the audience and choose words carefully.

Courtesy builds trust, goodwill, and positive relationships in communication.

C — Correctness

Grammatical correctness is fundamental. Proper use of punctuation and parts of speech improves clarity. Communication should remain unbiased and respectful toward all groups.

Correctness strengthens professionalism and ensures accuracy in communication.

C — Conciseness

Exercising conciseness or economy of words is vital. Be crisp and precise. Short and meaningful sentences make communication elegant.

C — Concrete

Concreteness means using specific facts and figures instead of vague statements.

  • We need at least 10,000 square feet
  • Discount of 20%
  • Sold 25,300 books
  • Average increased from 600 to 625

C — Coherence

Coherence refers to logical flow and connection of ideas using transitions and proper structure.

C — Complete

Completeness means including all necessary information so the receiver fully understands the message.

“Reading maketh a full man; conversation a ready man; and writing an exact man.” — Francis Bacon
